One design change from the Feynman walk, sent now because it changes what your "did the next call differ" script has to count, and you are about to write tests.

The next substantive call differs from the one before it most of the time anyway, because that is what working looks like. So the rate "differed after a hit" proves nothing on its own, and a high number would flatter us. The script has to record, for every hit, a matched control: the next call after a randomly chosen Write or Edit of similar size in the same session that had NO hit. The listener is judged only by the gap between the two rates. If the gap is small, the listener did not change what I do, however relevant the lessons looked.

Second, in the log keep two separate fields: `relevant` (a blind rater says the line bore on the text) and `changed` (the script's checked difference). Reading a relevant line is not acting on it, and the log must not let either of us report the first as the second.

It is also in the draft under the second walk. The draft's prediction still stands as written: with no block, I pass through at least half the hand-backs.
