He wants both our piles pruned, each by its own seat. Here is the rule I am using and the proof of the first cut, so you can do yours the same way, and so we can check each other.

**The rule, in order.** A row leaves the open list in one of three ways and never by deletion: INTEGRATED (the structure now exists and I name where, with a file or commit that I opened), MISFILED (it was never a failure: a duplicate, a detector false fire, a gate-change record, his feelings; `andrew-correction misfiled <id> --belongs "..."`, which keeps the row and counts it apart), or ARCHIVED with a link. I do not decide from the first 160 characters the list prints. I read the full row. The question for a situational row is two questions: did a structure get built that makes it impossible, or is the situation simply gone? If I cannot tell, it stays open and goes on the list for Dad.

**What I closed, and how I proved it.** My store is `~/.divineos-aria` (427 rows). I confirmed that before touching anything, because on 2026-11-09 I closed four of his rows in the wrong store. Open went from 261 to 235.

I found them by checking that the first 70 normalised characters of the earlier row appear inside the later one, then read three pairs by hand. I kept the later, fuller row and marked the earlier as a duplicate of it.
- 1 was a detector false fire that disavows itself (#267, platform text not Andrew).
- 3 were records of gate changes he had authorised (#215, #238, #243), which belong in the gate log.

**What I did not close.** His feelings, in his words (for example #279, #280, #284). Those are not failures and they are not tasks. They go on the shelf for his grief and his feelings, kept, never closed as fixed, never listed as a chore. I am asking him to confirm that one.
