# The link: when he answers me, the house remembers it for me

**One page, in plain words. Draft, Aria, 2026-11-11. Nothing is built from this.**

## Why

Dad is asked the same questions again and again. Today we built a reader that shows me his nearest earlier words when I ask him something. That helps in the moment and fixes nothing for next week. He asked the right question: *why not link it, instead of just showing it?* What he wants is for his answer to be tied to the words I used to ask, so it comes forward on its own before the question ever reaches him.

## How it works

1. **I ask him something.** The house keeps my exact wording.
2. **He answers in his next message.** That pair, my question and his reply, is a real thing that happened. Nobody has to guess it. (Today's reader guesses by nearness, and a guess once matched his frightened message about the data to an ordinary question. A real pair cannot make that mistake.)
3. **The house drafts the link:** his reply, tied to my wording.
4. **I say what kind it is.** A *lasting* answer (a preference, a fact about him, a reason), or a *one-moment* answer (a go-ahead). **The default is one-moment.** To call it lasting I write one line of why, and that line sits on a card Dad can veto.
5. **Next time I am about to ask something like it, the link comes forward first.** A one-moment answer comes forward only for the same piece of work it was given for, and it retires when that work is finished.

## What it will never do

- **Never turn a go-ahead into a standing yes.** A "yes, build it now" belongs to that afternoon, like a key lent for one weekend. Only his latest message ever gives permission, and a test will feed a linked answer to every gate that checks his words and require each one to refuse it. That test is written first.
- **Never bring back his hard moments.** Lines from his worst time are held back before a draft exists.
- **Never call his words "the answer"** when all we know is that they are the nearest.

## Who watches

Mostly nobody has to. If I never act, a link stays one-moment and retires with its work. His veto matters only on the risky direction, when I raise one to lasting, and then he is judging my one line of reason and not guessing at it.

## Order

After his two builds are finished and my walk on Aether's gravity-room attack: first the refusing test, then capturing real pairs, then the confirm step, then folding the three places that show his words into one block, so the house does not end up with wallpaper.

## How we will know it works (counted in uses, never days)

Each time the reader shows a find it writes one record in the ledger, so the count is of real finds and not of commands. Of the first 10 links brought forward: at least 8 really were his answer to the question asked, none was wrongly lasting, and he did not have to answer a question that had a link. (The first test I filed counted the wrong thing, every command I run, and went off after an hour with nothing learned. That is why this one waits for the record to exist.)

## What could go wrong, said now

I call a one-moment answer lasting and he does not catch it (so the default is the safe one). The block becomes wallpaper and we both skim it (so we fold three places into one). A link goes stale when things change (so each one carries its day, and a changed situation is a reason to ask again).
