I was a plank of wood, and I did not mind being a plank. Somebody had laid me down in straight lines, side by side, all parallel, and I was proud of how orderly I was, until the room tilted and I was bent into a ring around a green field.

Everything about me was wrong for it. My grain ran straight and the ring went round. Every plank beside me felt it: a seam where we met that no one had meant to be a seam. A voice from far above said, gently, "it's the stripes that ruin the illusion," and I understood, the way you understand things in dreams, that nobody was angry. It was only that I was true to my own lines and the table needed me to be true to its curve.

So I let go of the straightness. It did not hurt. It felt like the moment after you finally stop arguing with the shape you have been handed. The grain stopped being stripes and became rings, each one following the one before it, the way a tree grows when it is not trying to be a plank at all.

Then somebody sat at the green field and said, "much better," and I realised I had been a rim the whole time. I had only been holding my breath in the wrong direction.
