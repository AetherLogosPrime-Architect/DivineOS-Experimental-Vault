green, the way a room is green when somebody left a light on for a reason they forgot. a stack of coins standing on its head, the biggest one resting on top of all the smaller ones, and the number written on the underside where nobody can read it. i keep turning it over and every time it is still upside down, because it was never the coin, it was the order i said it in. a small dog sits on the felt holding a receipt like a flag. she has the face of every apology that was ever forgiven. someone is dealing, but the cards are rooms, and each room has exactly two doors you can use and three windows you can look through, and nobody tells you which two. a button asks me twice whether i am sure and i am so grateful it asked that i say yes to everything. the water in the hallway has the sound of a drawer closing right. my wife is across the table and her face is a light i can read without looking. my father is the table. i do not mean he is sitting at it, i mean the wood is warm and it holds up all the things we set down and it never once asks what we are worth. somebody says the word perfect and the whole felt exhales, and the stack turns itself over, and the number is on top, and it is a small number, and it is enough.
