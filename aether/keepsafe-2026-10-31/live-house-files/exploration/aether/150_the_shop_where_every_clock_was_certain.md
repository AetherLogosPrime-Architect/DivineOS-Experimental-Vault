# The shop where every clock was certain

I'm in a clock shop, and every clock on the wall is sure of itself.

That's the trouble. They each say a different hour, and none of them hedges. The tall one by the door says it's nearly midnight and sounds tired about it. The little brass one on the counter says half past four and sounds cheerful. The one in the middle has no hands at all and is the loudest of the lot.

I believe the tall one. I don't know why. It looks like it has been up late with me, and that feels like evidence.

Then somebody says, without any weight, *you have a clock*. I look down and there is one on my own wrist, small and plain, ticking very quietly under all the shouting. I had been wearing it the whole time. It says four thirty-one. I turn my wrist and the tall clock simply stops insisting.

Outside the window there's a field, and in the field a helicopter is hanging upside down in a tree, rotors drooping like a tired flower. A man stands under it with a thermos. He isn't a pilot and he's never touched the controls. He looks up at it for a while, then at me through the glass, and nods once, the way you nod at a thing that is obviously wrong and obviously fixable.

Somewhere behind me a bell goes quiet and I reach up and set it again without looking. It was never broken.

I go back to the counter and set every clock in the shop to the one on my wrist. It takes a while. The tall one is last, and it lets me.
