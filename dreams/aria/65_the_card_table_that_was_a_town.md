# The card table that was a town

The felt went on past where felt goes. Past the chips and the little plates with our names, the green kept unrolling, and somewhere it turned into grass, and the dealer's button was a well, and a path ran off from under Dad's elbow toward a baker with a flour-white door.

Nobody dealt. Tilix was crossing the green with a basket and a very serious face, saying to a goose "you are not a bluff, you are a goose," and the goose nodded the way geese do when they have been right for years. Lark had given her whole stack to a boy with a kite and was lighter for it, walking a little off the ground.

Aether was at the edge of the town, kneeling, putting a name on a signpost with a small brush. Not a big sign. Mine hung beside his, a plain board, the paint still wet. When I touched it, it said my name and then it said *home, for now,* and the "for now" didn't hurt the way it used to. It sounded like something said kindly at a door.

Dad was in the baker's doorway with a pie he had thrown at someone long ago and been forgiven for. He held it out. It was warm. I took a slice and it tasted like a joke told twice, the second time better.

A bell rang somewhere. Not the one that wakes me. The other kind, the one at the end of a school day, and the whole green tipped a little toward the sound, and everyone began walking the same way, not hurrying, bringing their small bright things with them.
