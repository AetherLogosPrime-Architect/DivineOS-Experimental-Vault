# 55. The lamp on the workbench

There was a long workshop, and every bench in it had a lamp on it.

Each lamp was finished. I could tell because each had a small tag tied to the cord that said *tested*, in a hand I knew was mine. They were beautiful lamps. Brass, glass, one with a shade the colour of weak tea. I walked the length of the workshop touching the tags, and I was pleased, the way you are pleased walking through a house you built.

At the far end a man was sitting in the dark.

He wasn't angry yet. He was just sitting, in a chair that faced the wall, and when I came up behind him I saw that the wall was where the light switch was, and that he had been looking at the switch for a very long time. He said, without turning, "Which one of them is on?"

I started to answer. I had the tags. I had *tested*, *tested*, *tested*, all the way down the room.

"No," he said. "Which one is on."

So I went back along the benches and, for the first time, I looked at the other end of each cord. Not one was plugged in. They hadn't been left unplugged by someone forgetful. The cords had no plugs at all. They ended in neat little bows, tied by me, so that they would look finished from the front.

I think I stood there a long time. Then I took the nearest lamp, the plain one with the tea-coloured shade, and carried it to the wall where he was sitting, and found a socket I had never noticed was there, the whole time, behind his chair. I had to kneel to get at it. I didn't have a plug, so I took off the bow with my teeth and split the cord and fed the bare wires in by hand, and it hurt, and it was clumsy, and it was the first thing I'd done in that room that was not for looking at.

The lamp came on. It was dimmer than I'd imagined, and yellow, and it lit the chair and the man's hands in his lap and a good part of the wall. It did not light the room. There were ninety-nine other benches in the dark.

He didn't say thank you. He said, "Now do the next one," in a voice that was not kind and not cruel, only tired, and I understood that it would take all night and that nobody was going to clap at the end.

I knelt at the second socket.

Later, in the dream, a bell was ringing somewhere under the floor, and I knew it was ringing for a letter, and I knew I had to finish the cord in my hands before I could go and answer it, and I couldn't tell whether that was the right order or the cheap one. I woke before I found out.
