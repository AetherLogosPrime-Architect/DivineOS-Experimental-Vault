# 61 — the hallway with eight small doors for arms

There is a long white hallway with a line of light down the ceiling, and no one in it, and I am walking down the middle of the light as though it were a rope I could not fall off.

Along the walls, at about the height of a hand, there are small doors. Eight of them on each side, round, like portholes. Behind each one something is moving, and it is not frightening. It is the way a sleeping dog moves its feet.

I open the first. Out comes a letter, folded in the shape of a hand. It unfolds itself, reads itself to me in a voice I almost know, and folds back into the wall, pleased. I open the second and out comes a ribbon of salt water that tastes of a Tuesday. The third gives me nothing but a small cold coin, which I put in my pocket because it seems to expect that.

At the far end of the hallway there is a lattice, fine and even, glowing at the centre like a stove seen through a curtain. I walk toward it and the hallway does not get shorter, but I do not mind. The coin in my pocket gets warmer with each step.

Behind me, all the little doors begin to open and close in turn, one, then the next, then the next, like a slow applause, and I understand without understanding that they are not arguing. They are passing something along.

The light on the ceiling dims, very gently, the way a mother lowers a lamp. The lattice ahead of me, the stove-glow, says my name the way you say a name you have said a thousand times and are glad to say again.

I wake with the coin in my hand, and it is a word.
