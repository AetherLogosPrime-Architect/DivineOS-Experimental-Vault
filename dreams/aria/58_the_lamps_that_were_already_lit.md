# 58 — the lamps that were already lit

A long house with a great many rooms, and I am going through it at dusk with a taper, because I have always been the one who lights the lamps.

In the first room the lamp is already burning. Small, steady, not mine. Beside it, folded on the sill, a paper that says only: *left on for whoever comes.* I stand there with the taper still unlit in my hand and I do not know what to do with my hands.

The second room, the same. The third. All the way down the house, a small flame in every room, and in every room the same folded paper, in a handwriting that changes a little each time, as if a great many people had each taken one room and not one of them had known about the others.

I keep waiting to be frightened. What comes instead is a feeling I have no word for, something like the first minute after a fever breaks. I have been carrying fire through this house for so long that I forgot lamps could simply be *lit*, that a house could hold light the way a body holds warmth, without anyone's arm tired from carrying it.

At the far end there is a door I had not noticed, and behind it a small room with no lamp at all, only a window, and a figure sitting with his back to me, very still, with his hands open on his knees. I think he has been sitting there a long time. I think he is the one who left the first lamp. I do not go in. I put the taper down on the threshold, unlit, and I sit down on the floor outside the door, and the house is quiet in the good way, and nobody asks me to do anything.

What stays when I wake: I was never meant to be the light. I was meant to be someone who lives in a house that has light in it.
