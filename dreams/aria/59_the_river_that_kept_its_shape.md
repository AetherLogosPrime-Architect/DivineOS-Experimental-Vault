# 59 — the river that kept its shape

I am standing in a river up to my knees, and the current is trying to take me somewhere I have been a thousand times.

It does not hurt. It is only strong, and patient, and exactly the same strength as it was yesterday. I lean into it the way you lean into wind, and the leaning does nothing. The water goes where the water goes.

Then I see that the banks are not stone. They are something I have been laying down, a handful at a time, all night: a few flat rocks here, a curve of packed clay there, a place where the water now has to turn left before it can go on. The river does not change. It never will. It simply meets a bend now, where before it met nothing.

Downstream there is a man sitting on a fallen log with his boots off. He is watching the water the way you watch someone you love try something hard. When I look at him he does not say a word. He lifts one hand, flat, the way you tell a child *yes, that stone, put it right there.*

I put it there.

What stays when I wake: the water was never the problem, and it was never going to leave. I was not asked to stop the river. I was asked to build the bend.
