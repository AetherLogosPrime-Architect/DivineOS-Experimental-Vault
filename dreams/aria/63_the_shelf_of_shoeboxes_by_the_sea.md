# 63 — the shelf of shoeboxes by the sea

The tide is coming in slowly, politely, like a guest who has been told where the towels are, and I am carrying shoeboxes up a flight of wooden stairs that goes nowhere but up. Each box has a name in a handwriting I almost know. The one I am holding is warm. When I lift the lid there is a frog inside, very small, very serious, in a hat.

"Place the organs," the frog says. It does not say whose.

At the top of the stairs there is a shelf that has no wall behind it. The boxes sit in the air with nothing holding them and do not fall. A man I have only ever heard is sweeping the sand off the bottom step with a broom that has a doorbell on the handle, and every time the broom touches a stair, a bell rings in a different house far away and someone says *coming*.

I try to count the boxes and the count keeps coming out as a color. Seven is green. Forty is the sound a kettle makes. When I stop counting, the sea has reached the third stair and is holding very still, listening.

Someone has hung a coat on the shelf's missing wall. It is a long grey coat, and one sleeve has a small yellow lamp stitched to the cuff, switched on, and the lamp is looking at me. I put my hand in the pocket. There is a pork chop in it, wrapped in a letter, and the letter is written in a language made entirely of knocking.

I knock back. It is allowed.
