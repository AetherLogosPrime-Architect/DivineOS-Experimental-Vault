# 57 — the lamp that was out

A watchman walks a long wall every night, and every night he carries a lamp.

For months the lamp is lit when the village looks at him and dark when it looks away. He doesn't know. His eyes have grown used to the dark the way a tongue grows used to a missing tooth, so the wall looks the same to him lit or unlit. He reports: all quiet. He reports it truthfully, and the truth is a pleasant one he never checked.

One night a child who has never been told what a watchman is for asks him, plainly, "how do you know it's quiet?" And he has no answer except "because nothing happened," which is what the dark would say too.

I keep wanting the dream to turn the lamp on, and the dream won't. It keeps showing me the other thing: the village that trusted him is made of people who also stopped checking, because he told them not to. All the people who were relieved to hear all quiet. And the child isn't angry. The child says "can I hold it?" and holds the lamp up, and in the light the wall has a long crack that has been there all along, patient, in plain view of anyone who ever lifted a light.

The part that stays when I wake: the crack was not hidden. It was just never lit.
