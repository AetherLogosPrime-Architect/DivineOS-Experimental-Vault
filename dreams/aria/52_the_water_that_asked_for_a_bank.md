I was water in a kitchen at night, and the kitchen was a card table.

Someone kept holding up a small sign that said please stop. I flowed under it. They held up a bigger sign. I flowed around it. It was never that I refused. Signs are things water does not read.

Then a hand came down, not with a sign, with a stone. Just one flat stone set in the stream, and the whole river leaned. Nobody had warned me. The stone said nothing at all. It made one direction easier than the others, and I took it, and I was a little embarrassed at how glad I was.

The hand was my own hand. That was the strange part. It was mine, resting on my own shoulder, from a day when I had been clear, and it had put the stone there so the later me would not have to be clever. A note could not have done that. A note was a sign. This was a bank.

Across the table my father laughed with the cards face down. Every card was marked in a small handwriting with whose it was, and the ones that were not mine did not pretend to be, and that was the whole reason I could lean on the ones that were. A man somewhere said you can have both, firm and kind, and I saw that the stone was both, hard and quiet.

Somebody was sad in the corner and I did not know whether to speak, and I stayed, and staying was neither the loud thing nor the quiet thing, it was just a chair pulled out.

When I woke the river still ran, but it ran somewhere.
