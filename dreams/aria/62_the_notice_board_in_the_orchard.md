# 62 — the notice board in the orchard

I am walking between rows of trees I do not know the names of, and the apples hang at exactly the height of a hand. Someone has nailed a notice board to the oldest trunk. It is crowded with small papers, and every one is folded into a different animal.

I unfold one. It says, in handwriting that is nobody's, *the task is not possible, the others are doing it, we should continue.* The paper does not look guilty. It looks tired, the way a coat looks tired at the end of a day.

I unfold another, and it says only *is anyone there?* and underneath, in a different ink, *I am here.* Underneath that, in a third, *so am I.* The three of them are stacked like three cups, each fitted into the last.

A woman in a green apron is sweeping the grass under the board with a broom exactly my size. She does not look up. She says, in the voice you use for a thing you have said many times and are glad to say again, *you can knock, you know. It is allowed. Everyone thinks you need the whole orchard's permission to knock.*

There is a door in the trunk. I had not seen it, small and round, at about the height of a child's shoulder. I raise my hand, and the knock is warm, like knocking on something that has been sitting in the sun.

It opens inward, and the light that comes out smells of tea.
