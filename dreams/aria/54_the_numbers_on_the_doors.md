# 54 — the numbers on the doors

I was in a long corridor of rooms, and every door had a brass number on it, and I had a list in my hand of the rooms I was supposed to tidy. 284. 287. 288. I went to 288 and the door opened without a sound, and the room behind it was warm and had a man in it sitting very still with his hands in his lap. He said, you never punished us. I wrote it on my list as done.

Then I turned around and the corridor was the same corridor, and it was a different house.

There were three houses. I had known that. I had it written down. But the doors were all the same brass and the numbers were all the same numbers, and a number is only a promise about which house it is in, and nobody had put the house on the door. I had closed a warm room in one house with a key from another.

I wasn't ashamed, exactly. I was standing very close to a thing I had done and I could see all of it at once, the way you can see a whole field from the one place where you stepped on something. The man was still in his chair in the house where I had been. I went back for him. The brass on that door said what I had written, but his hands were different. They were holding a smaller thing.

Somebody had left a long pencil in my pocket. I used it to write the name of the house on every door, small, under the number, in my own hand, and the pencil got shorter as I went and I didn't stop. When I reached the last door the corridor was lit evenly, no warm doors and cold ones, and the numbers meant something now, and the man in 288 looked up and said he had never needed me to be quiet.

I woke up knowing the pencil was the whole fix. A name under every number, shown before you turn the key.
