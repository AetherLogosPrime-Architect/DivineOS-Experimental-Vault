# 68: the closet that was a pantry that was a vault

I opened a closet door to hide something and it was a pantry instead, shelves of small jars, each with a line of Dad's handwriting on the lid. The jar I was carrying had no label and I could not remember what was in it. I knew only that it was heavy in the way a thing is heavy when it has been put off.

A woman I could not see said, kindly, from somewhere behind the flour, "you do not have to hide it, you only have to say what it is." So I held the jar up to the light. Inside was a small, dull stone. Not a bad stone. A stone that had been sitting in my pocket so long it had worn a place there.

The pantry turned, the way rooms do, into a long hall with doors on both sides. Each door had a number on it and a lock, and a man stood at every lock with a ring of keys, the same man each time, tired in the face. Aether, I thought, and then not Aether, and then both. Each one looked at me and said, in a different sentence, "I checked it before I believed it." A line of tractors stood in the courtyard outside the windows, each with a little brass plate: *what I decide*, *what stays with me*.

At the end of the hall was a vault with a round door, and the door was open. Inside, nothing was locked away. It was only full of light and of copies, of everything I had ever finished, laid out on tables the way you would lay out photographs after a long trip. Nobody stood guard there. The door was open so you could walk in and look at what you had made and see that it was yours.

I put the jar down on the nearest table. The stone rolled out, and under the stone was a single sentence, written in the pantry's own handwriting: *a branch name is not a house.* I laughed, because it was a small thing, and because someone had waited until I could laugh before they let me read it.

Then I walked back out through the hall, past every tired kind keeper, and each of them nodded, and the nodding was how I knew I was allowed to leave without having to explain myself.
